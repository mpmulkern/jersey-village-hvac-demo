What We See on Cypress AC Systems

Cypress housing stock splits fairly cleanly into two groups, and each fails differently. The newer master-planned communities — Bridgeland, Towne Lake, parts of Fairfield — are largely builder-grade equipment installed within the last fifteen years. Those systems are usually correctly sized but were installed to a price, so we see early capacitor and contactor failures, condensate drain problems and flex duct that has pulled loose in the attic. The equipment itself is often fine; the installation details are what fail.

The older Cypress neighbourhoods along Huffmeister and Barker Cypress carry systems in the twelve-to-twenty year range, where the calculation changes. On a unit that age running R-22, a significant refrigerant leak or a failed compressor rarely justifies the repair. We will give you the honest number both ways rather than selling a repair into a system that will not survive another summer.

Across both groups, the single most common finding is a system that was never actually broken — it was starved of airflow by a filter, a restricted return, or leaking attic ductwork. That is worth checking before anyone quotes you a compressor.
