What We See on Spring AC Systems

Spring covers a wide span of housing age, and the failures track it closely. The 1970s and 1980s neighbourhoods around Louetta, Stuebner Airline and Cypresswood carry mature tree cover and, frequently, systems well past fifteen years. Those calls are usually compressor fatigue, badly corroded evaporator coils, and duct systems that have been patched rather than repaired. The shade is pleasant but it also drops leaf litter and seed into outdoor condenser coils, which quietly raises head pressure all summer.

North and west toward Gleannloch Farms, Augusta Pines and Spring Trails, the equipment is newer and the pattern flips. Those systems are typically builder-installed, correctly sized on paper, and let down by installation detail: flex duct pulled loose in the attic, returns too small for the tonnage, condensate lines run without a proper trap. We find plenty of ten-year-old systems in these neighbourhoods that have never cooled the upstairs properly, and the cause is airflow rather than the air conditioner.

Across all of Spring, attic air handlers are the norm, and that is where the expensive surprises live. A blocked condensate drain in a Spring attic does not just stop the AC — it puts water through the ceiling below. We check the drain, trap and secondary pan on every attic system we open, whatever the original complaint was.
