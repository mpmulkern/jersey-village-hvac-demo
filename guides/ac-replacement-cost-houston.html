AC Replacement Cost in Houston: 2026 Guide

Replacing an air conditioning system is one of the larger unplanned expenses a Houston homeowner meets, and the quotes that arrive rarely explain themselves. This guide sets out what a new system actually costs across the Houston metro in 2026, what moves the number up or down, and how to read three competing estimates that all claim to be for the same job. Prices below reflect complete installed systems in Jersey Village and the surrounding suburbs, not equipment-only figures.

Quick Answer: What should I budget?

Most Houston homes land between $4,500 and $9,500 for a complete replacement. A three-ton builder-grade system in an accessible attic sits near the bottom; a five-ton high-efficiency system with duct corrections and a difficult install sits at the top. Anything quoted far below that range is usually missing the coil, the line set, or the permit.

Cost by System Size

Tonnage is the biggest single driver, and in the Houston metro it tracks conditioned square footage more loosely than the old rules of thumb suggest, because glazing, attic insulation and duct condition matter enormously here. As a working guide, a typical 1,500 square foot Jersey Village home needs around three tons, 2,000 to 2,400 square feet usually lands on four, and larger two-storey homes in Cypress or Katy commonly need five tons or a zoned arrangement.

At standard 14.3 SEER2 efficiency, a complete three-ton changeout generally runs $4,500 to $6,000 installed. Four tons lands around $5,500 to $7,500, and five tons $6,500 to $9,000. Those figures assume a straight replacement of both the indoor and outdoor units, a new evaporator coil, a new drain pan and float switch, permit, disposal of the old equipment, and a correctly evacuated and weighed-in charge.

Stepping up to roughly 16 to 17 SEER2 typically adds $1,200 to $2,500 depending on size. Premium variable-speed and communicating systems add considerably more, often $3,000 to $6,000 over the base figure, and are bought mainly for humidity control and quiet running rather than for the electricity saving alone.

What Pushes a Houston Quote Up

  • Attic access that will not take a full-size cased coil without structural work
  • Duct systems past their service life, adding roughly $800 to $2,500 for partial repair
  • Returns undersized for the new tonnage, requiring new return drops or grilles
  • An R-22 system, where the line set must be flushed or replaced rather than reused
  • Electrical work such as a new disconnect, correct breaker sizing or a service upgrade
  • Condensate re-piping where the original drain was never trapped or sloped properly
  • Zoning or a second system for a two-storey home that has never cooled evenly

Why Houston Efficiency Maths Differs

Efficiency upgrades pay back faster here than almost anywhere in the country, and the reason is simply running hours. Our cooling season stretches from March into November, so a system in Jersey Village accumulates far more operating time each year than the same equipment in a four-season climate. A percentage saving applied across eight months is a materially different proposition to the same percentage applied across four.

Humidity control is the second half of the argument, and it rarely appears in the quote. A variable-speed system runs longer at lower capacity, which removes considerably more moisture than a single-stage unit cycling on and off. In a Houston August that difference is felt directly: the same thermostat setting is comfortable at a lower relative humidity, and the house does not develop the clammy feel that comes with short-cycling equipment.

Comparing Estimates Fairly

The single most useful thing you can do with three quotes is check whether they describe the same scope. A quote that reuses the existing evaporator coil is not comparable to one that replaces it, and pairing a new condenser with an old coil frequently voids the manufacturer warranty besides running below its rated efficiency. The same applies to the line set, the drain pan and float switch, the permit, and whether the charge is weighed in or set by pressure.

Ask each contractor what load calculation they performed. Sizing by replacing whatever was there before perpetuates an original error, and an oversized system in our humidity cools the air quickly, shuts off before removing the moisture, and leaves the house cold and clammy. Oversizing is a more common fault locally than undersizing.

If the system is failing rather than failed, it is also worth establishing whether replacement is the right call at all. Our repair-versus-replacement guide sets out where that line falls, and a great many quotes triggered by a dead outdoor unit turn out to be capacitor or contactor faults rather than compressor failures.

Protecting the Investment

A new system inherits whatever killed the old one unless those conditions are corrected. That means the duct system assessed rather than assumed, the condensate drain re-piped with a proper trap and a working float switch, and filter sizing set so the equipment gets the airflow it was designed for. From there, a seasonal maintenance visit is what keeps a fifteen-year system from becoming a ten-year one. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a new AC system cost in Houston in 2026?

A complete residential system replacement in the Houston metro generally runs $4,500 to $9,500 installed. A three-ton 14.3 SEER2 system in an accessible attic sits near the bottom of that range; a five-ton high-efficiency variable-speed system with duct modifications sits at the top or beyond it.

Is a higher SEER2 system worth the extra money in Houston?

More often here than in most of the country, because our cooling season is roughly eight months long and the efficiency gain applies across all of it. Moving from 14.3 to around 17 SEER2 typically pays back within six to nine years on an average Houston bill, while the jump to premium variable-speed equipment is usually bought for comfort and humidity control rather than payback.

Why are two quotes for the same house thousands apart?

Usually because they are not the same job. Check whether each includes a new evaporator coil or reuses the old one, a new line set or a flush, permit and disposal, a replacement drain pan and float switch, and any duct or return work. A cheap quote that reuses a twelve-year-old coil is a different piece of work, not a better price.

Should I replace in winter to get a better price?

There is a modest advantage. Houston demand peaks from May to September, and scheduling is easier and pricing marginally softer in the cooler months. It is not a large enough saving to justify nursing a failing system through another August, but if you already know replacement is coming, planning it for the off-season is sensible.

Does my ductwork need replacing at the same time?

Not always, but it needs assessing. Original flex duct in a 1980s Houston attic is often past its service life, and a new system connected to leaking or undersized ducts will not deliver its rated capacity. Partial duct repair is common and typically adds $800 to $2,500, while a full replacement is a larger project.
