AC Refrigerant Leak Detection & Repair in Jersey Village, TX

An air conditioner that cools poorly in the afternoon, ices up on the copper line, or runs constantly without ever reaching the thermostat setting is usually telling you the same thing: the refrigerant charge is low. Jersey Village HVAC Pros locates the leak, repairs it where repair makes financial sense, and recharges the system to the manufacturer's weighed-in specification rather than guessing by gauge pressure.

Quick Answer: Why does my AC keep losing refrigerant?

Because a sealed system does not use refrigerant up. If the charge is low, it escaped somewhere — most often a corroded evaporator coil, a vibration-worn line set, or a leaking Schrader valve. Adding more without finding the leak fixes the symptom for a few weeks and leaves the cause running.

Where Houston Systems Actually Leak

Four failure points account for the large majority of what we find in Harris County homes. The evaporator coil in the attic air handler is first and worst: Houston's humidity keeps that coil wet for months at a time, and formic acid from ordinary household off-gassing pits the aluminium and copper until pinholes form. Coils in the eight-to-fifteen year range are the classic case, and the leak is usually diffuse rather than a single fixable spot.

Second is the line set running from the attic or closet down to the condenser outside. Where it passes through framing or sits against a strap, decades of compressor vibration rub through the insulation and eventually the copper. Third are the service valves and Schrader cores at the outdoor unit, which are cheap to replace and are worth checking before anyone opens a wall. Fourth are brazed joints at the condenser, particularly on systems that were installed in a hurry.

Knowing which of those four you have changes the economics completely, which is why the search comes before the quote.

How We Find the Leak

  • Verify the charge is genuinely low by measuring superheat and subcooling, not by pressure alone — a restricted filter or dirty coil mimics low charge
  • Electronic heated-diode detection along the coil, line set, joints and service valves
  • UV dye injection for slow seasonal leaks that will not show on a single visit
  • Nitrogen pressure test on the isolated section when the leak is suspected but not confirmed
  • Written findings with the repair cost and the replacement cost side by side before any work starts

Repair, or Replace the System?

An accessible line-set repair or a valve core is straightforward and worth doing on almost any system. A leaking evaporator coil is the decision point. On a system under ten years old, a coil replacement returns a machine with real life left in it. On a fifteen-year-old unit, the same coil job costs a meaningful fraction of a new correctly sized system that will cut summer runtime and come with a fresh warranty.

Systems still running R-22 push that calculation further still. Production and import ended in 2020, so the only supply is reclaimed, and the price per pound reflects that. We give you both numbers in writing and let you decide, rather than quoting a repair that quietly commits you to the same conversation next summer. If replacement is the answer, our AC replacement page covers sizing and financing, and the repair-versus-replacement guide walks through the arithmetic in detail.

Why an Undercharged System Is Urgent

Running low on charge is not a slow, harmless decline. The compressor depends on returning refrigerant vapour for cooling, so an undercharged system runs the compressor hot and shortens its life measurably. Low charge also drops coil temperature below freezing, so the evaporator ices over, airflow collapses, and the melt cycle dumps water into the attic pan or through the ceiling. A leak found in May is a repair; the same leak in August is often an emergency call with water damage attached.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you just add refrigerant instead of finding the leak?

We can, and sometimes that is the right short-term call during a heat advisory. But refrigerant is not consumed by a sealed system, so a low charge always means a leak. Topping off without finding it means paying again in weeks or months, and running low on charge is what burns out compressors.

How much does refrigerant leak repair cost in the Houston area?

A leak search with electronic detection typically runs $150 to $350. An accessible line-set or Schrader-valve repair plus recharge usually lands between $400 and $900. An evaporator coil replacement is the expensive case at roughly $1,200 to $2,600, which is where replacing an older system often makes more sense.

Is a refrigerant leak dangerous to my family?

Modern R-410A is non-toxic and non-flammable in residential quantities, so a slow leak is an efficiency and equipment problem rather than a household health hazard. The real risks are compressor damage from running undercharged and a frozen coil dumping water into the attic or ceiling.

My system uses R-22. Is it worth repairing?

Rarely. R-22 has not been produced or imported since 2020, and reclaimed stock now costs several times what R-410A does. On an R-22 system with a significant leak, the recharge alone can approach a meaningful share of a new system, so replacement is usually the better value.
