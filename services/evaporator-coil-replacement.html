Evaporator Coil Replacement & Cleaning in Jersey Village, TX

The evaporator coil is the part of your air conditioner that actually removes heat and humidity from the house, and in Houston it works harder and stays wetter than almost anywhere in the country. When it ices over, corrodes through, or clogs with the fine dust that gets past a cheap filter, the whole system stops cooling properly. Jersey Village HVAC Pros diagnoses coil problems accurately, cleans what can be cleaned, and replaces what cannot.

Quick Answer: Is a frozen coil an emergency?

It needs attention today, though not usually at midnight. Turn the cooling off and leave the fan running to thaw the ice, and put a towel under the air handler. Running a frozen system risks slugging liquid refrigerant back to the compressor, which turns a moderate repair into a system replacement.

Why Houston Coils Fail Early

Our cooling season runs roughly eight months, and for most of that time the coil sits below the dew point with condensate running across it. A coil in Jersey Village is simply wet for far more hours per year than the same coil in a drier climate. Constant moisture plus formic acid, which forms from ordinary off-gassing of carpets, adhesives, cleaning products and building materials, slowly pits the aluminium fins and copper tubing until pinholes appear.

That is why coil leaks here tend to be diffuse rather than a single repairable spot, and why ten to fifteen years is a realistic service life locally against the fifteen-to-twenty often quoted nationally. Attic installation makes it worse, because summer attic temperatures accelerate the chemistry and the coil lives above your ceiling drywall.

Symptoms We Investigate

  • Ice visible on the copper line set or the coil itself, followed by poor cooling once it melts
  • Water stains on the ceiling below the attic air handler, or a full secondary drain pan
  • The system runs continuously but the house holds several degrees above setpoint
  • Musty smell when the blower starts, pointing to biological growth on a dirty wet coil
  • Charge that has to be topped up every season — a classic corroded-coil signature

Cleaning Versus Replacing

Plenty of coils we are called to look at are structurally fine and simply filthy. A matted layer of dust on the upstream face acts as insulation and blocks airflow, producing exactly the weak-cooling and freeze-up symptoms of a failing system. A proper pull-and-clean, rather than a spray through the access panel, restores that coil for a fraction of replacement cost and takes a few hours.

Replacement is the answer when the coil is leaking. We confirm that first with electronic leak detection rather than assuming, because a low charge can also come from the line set or service valves, which are much cheaper to fix. When a cased coil does need replacing, we match it to the existing condenser so the pairing stays within manufacturer specification, pull a deep vacuum, and weigh the new charge in rather than setting it by pressure.

If the condenser outside is also near end of life, we will say so plainly. Putting a new coil on a tired fifteen-year-old system is how homeowners end up paying for the same job twice, and our repair-versus-replacement guide sets out where that line usually falls.

Protecting the New Coil

A replacement coil lasts as long as the conditions allow, so we correct what killed the old one: filter sizing and a realistic change interval, a clear and properly trapped condensate drain, correct blower speed for the duct system, and any duct leakage pulling humid attic air into the return. A seasonal maintenance visit catches drain blockages and airflow restrictions before they freeze the coil again.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my evaporator coil keep freezing up?

A coil freezes when it gets colder than it should, which happens for two reasons: not enough warm air moving across it, or not enough refrigerant in it. Restricted filters, closed vents, a failing blower and collapsed flex duct cover the airflow side; a leak covers the other. Both are diagnosable in one visit.

How much does evaporator coil replacement cost in Houston?

A cased coil replacement in the Houston metro typically runs $1,200 to $2,600 installed, depending on tonnage, coil type and how accessible the attic air handler is. Deep cleaning an intact coil is far cheaper at roughly $300 to $500.

How long should an evaporator coil last in Houston humidity?

Ten to fifteen years is typical here, shorter than the national average because our coils stay wet for a much longer cooling season. Formic acid corrosion from ordinary household off-gassing is the usual end, producing pinhole leaks rather than a single failure point.

Should I replace just the coil or the whole system?

If the outdoor condenser is under about ten years old and in good condition, replacing the coil alone is sound. If the system is older, uses R-22, or the compressor is already weak, putting a new coil on it usually means paying twice within a few years.
