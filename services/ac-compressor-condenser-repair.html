AC Compressor & Condenser Repair in Jersey Village, TX

The condensing unit outside your house does the hard part of air conditioning: it rejects into the Houston afternoon all the heat the indoor coil pulls out of your rooms. It is also the component sitting in direct sun, in our humidity, collecting grass clippings and cottonwood seed all season. Jersey Village HVAC Pros repairs outdoor units properly, which starts with establishing whether the compressor itself has failed or whether something much cheaper is stopping it from running.

Quick Answer: Is a bad compressor worth repairing?

It depends almost entirely on the system's age. Under about eight years, a compressor is often still under manufacturer parts warranty and worth replacing. Past twelve years, especially on an R-22 system, the labour and refrigerant cost of a compressor swap approaches half the price of a new matched system that will run at far lower cost.

What Actually Fails on Houston Condensing Units

A long cooling season means our outdoor units accumulate running hours faster than they would in most of the country, and the failures follow a predictable order. Run capacitors go first and most often, because electrolytic components degrade with heat and ours spend eight months in a metal cabinet in full sun. A weak capacitor shows up as a compressor that hums and trips, or a fan that needs a push to start.

Contactors are next. The contacts pit and weld from repeated switching, and Houston's humidity accelerates the corrosion. Then condenser fan motors, whose bearings dry out and seize, usually taking out the capacitor on the way. The compressor itself is well down that list, which is exactly why we test in that order rather than announcing a compressor failure on the first visit.

Dirty condenser coils sit behind more of these failures than homeowners expect. A coil packed with dust and lawn debris cannot reject heat, so head pressure climbs, amp draw rises, and every electrical component in the cabinet runs hotter than it was designed to. A great many premature compressor failures we see started as a coil nobody rinsed.

Our Diagnostic Process

  • Measure incoming voltage and confirm the disconnect and breaker are sound before condemning anything
  • Test the run and start capacitors against their rated microfarads, not by appearance
  • Check contactor coil operation and contact resistance under load
  • Read compressor winding resistance and megohm the windings to ground to confirm an electrical failure
  • Take suction and liquid pressures with superheat and subcooling before touching the charge
  • Inspect the coil, fan blade and cabinet for airflow restriction and physical damage

Keeping the Outdoor Unit Alive

Most of what shortens condenser life is preventable. Keep two feet of clear space around the cabinet, rinse the coil from the inside out with a garden hose a couple of times each season, and replace filters on schedule so the indoor side is not starving the system of airflow. A seasonal maintenance visit catches a weakening capacitor or a dragging fan motor while it is still a small repair, and if the unit fails on a heat advisory weekend our emergency service covers it. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a failed compressor always a full system replacement?

No. Plenty of compressors that will not start are electrically sound and held back by a failed run capacitor, a burnt contactor or a weak start winding, all of which are ordinary repairs. A compressor that has genuinely gone to ground or seized is a different matter, and on a system over twelve years old replacement is usually the better spend.

Why does my outdoor unit trip the breaker on hot afternoons?

High head pressure raises the current the compressor draws, so a unit that is marginal at 80 degrees can exceed its breaker rating at 100. The usual causes are a dirty condenser coil, a failing condenser fan motor and an overcharged system. Repeated resetting of the breaker is worth avoiding because each locked-rotor start stresses the windings further.

How much does condenser fan motor replacement cost in Houston?

A condenser fan motor with a matched capacitor typically runs $350 to $700 installed in the Houston metro, depending on horsepower and whether the manufacturer part or a correctly specified universal motor is used. Replacing the capacitor at the same time is standard practice, not an upsell.

My outdoor fan is not spinning but the unit hums. What is wrong?

That combination points to a failed run capacitor or a seized fan motor bearing. Shut the system off at the thermostat and the breaker rather than leaving it humming, because the compressor is still trying to run with no heat rejection and will overheat within minutes.
