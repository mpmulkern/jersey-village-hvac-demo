Ductwork Repair & Sealing in Jersey Village, TX

A correctly sized air conditioner cannot cool a house through bad ductwork. In Jersey Village and across the Houston metro, most cooling complaints we diagnose are not compressor failures at all: they are leaking, crushed or undersized ducts sitting in an attic that reaches 130°F on an August afternoon. Jersey Village HVAC Pros inspects, seals, repairs and replaces residential and light commercial duct systems so the cooling you already pay for actually reaches the rooms you live in.

Quick Answer: How much cooling do leaky ducts waste?

A typical leaky residential duct system loses 20–30% of conditioned air before it reaches the vents. In a Houston-area home running an AC eight months a year, that is the difference between a system that keeps up in July and one that runs non-stop and still leaves the back bedrooms warm.

Duct Problems We Repair

Disconnected & Torn Flex Duct

Attic flex duct pulls loose at the boot or tears on framing, dumping cold air into the attic instead of the room.

Leaking Joints & Plenums

Failed mastic and dried-out tape at the plenum and branch takeoffs, sealed properly with mastic rather than duct tape.

Crushed or Kinked Runs

Compressed flex behind storage or sharp bends that choke airflow to one part of the house.

Undersized Returns

Too little return air raises static pressure, freezes coils and shortens compressor life. We resize and add returns.

Why Houston-Area Ductwork Fails Early

Duct systems in this region work under conditions most of the country never sees. Attic temperatures climb far above outdoor ambient for months at a time, which breaks down tape adhesives, hardens flex duct jackets and makes insulation sag away from the inner liner. Humidity does the rest: air leaking out of a supply duct is replaced by humid attic air pulled into the return side, so the system spends capacity dehumidifying air it should never have touched. Add the settling and framing movement common in the expansive clay soils under Jersey Village, Cypress and Katy, and connections that were tight at installation work themselves loose within a decade.

The result is a system that looks healthy on a service call — correct refrigerant charge, clean coil, healthy capacitor — but cannot hold the house at setpoint when it matters. Sealing and repairing the distribution side is usually the cheapest capacity you can buy, and it is almost always cheaper than the larger replacement system a homeowner is otherwise talked into.

Our Duct Inspection & Sealing Process

  • Room-by-room airflow and temperature readings to find where delivery is short
  • Static pressure test at the air handler to confirm restriction and return sizing
  • Full attic inspection of every accessible run, boot, takeoff and plenum connection
  • Written scope and price before work starts, with repair versus replacement laid out plainly
  • Mastic sealing at joints, mechanical repair of torn or disconnected runs, insulation restored
  • Post-repair airflow verification so the improvement is measured, not assumed

Repair, Seal or Replace?

Not every duct system needs replacing. If the layout is sound, the runs are correctly sized and the material is intact, sealing and targeted repair will recover most of the lost capacity for a fraction of the cost. Replacement makes sense when flex duct has deteriorated throughout, when the original layout starves parts of the house regardless of sealing, or when a new system is going in and the existing ducts cannot carry its airflow. We will tell you which case you are in, and why, before you commit to anything. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does duct sealing cost in Jersey Village?

Sealing accessible duct joints and repairing a handful of leaks in the Jersey Village and Houston area typically runs $450–$1,800. A full duct replacement for an average-sized home runs $2,800–$6,500 depending on layout, attic access and the amount of flex versus rigid duct. Jersey Village HVAC Pros inspects and prices the work before anything begins.

How do I know if my ducts are leaking?

The usual signs are rooms that never reach the thermostat setting, an AC that runs constantly through the afternoon, dust that returns days after cleaning, and a summer electric bill noticeably higher than neighbours with similar homes. A static pressure and airflow test confirms it.

Can leaky ducts damage my AC system?

Yes. Restricted or leaking return ducts starve the evaporator coil of airflow, which causes coil freezing, longer run times and premature compressor wear. Duct problems are one of the most common reasons a correctly sized system still fails early in Houston heat.
