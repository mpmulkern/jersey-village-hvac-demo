AC Condensate Drain Repair & Clearing in Jersey Village, TX

An air conditioner running through a Houston August pulls a startling amount of water out of the air, and every drop of it has to leave the house through a thin PVC line. When that line blocks, the water finds somewhere else to go, which in most Jersey Village homes means the ceiling under the attic air handler. Jersey Village HVAC Pros clears blocked condensate drains, corrects the piping faults that cause repeat blockages, and replaces the safety switches that stop a clog from becoming a claim.

Quick Answer: My AC stopped and there is water in the pan. What now?

Leave the system off. A float switch has shut it down deliberately because the drain is blocked, and that switch is the only thing between a clogged line and water through your ceiling. Vacuum the standing water from the pan if you can reach it safely, and book a drain clear the same day rather than bypassing the switch.

Why Houston Drains Block So Reliably

A three-ton system working in our humidity can remove well over ten gallons of water on a hot, sticky day. That water runs continuously across a coil that sits in the dark at around 40 degrees, which is an excellent environment for the biological slime that eventually plugs the line. Add the dust that gets past a filter, and the slime binds into a plug that no amount of running will push through.

Our cooling season compounds it. A drain in a milder climate carries water for perhaps four months; in Jersey Village it runs from March to November. The line simply never dries out and never gets a chance to stop growing. This is why an annual clear is the sensible local minimum rather than the over-servicing it might look like elsewhere.

Installation detail decides how badly it ends. A line without a proper trap lets the blower pull air through the drain instead of letting water flow, so the pan holds water even when the line is clear. A line with a sag holds standing water permanently. A secondary pan with no float switch, or a switch that was disconnected by a previous tech, removes the safety net entirely.

Signs Your Drain Needs Attention

  • A brown ring or damp patch on the ceiling below the attic air handler
  • The system runs, then shuts off by itself and restarts hours later
  • Standing water in the primary or secondary drain pan
  • Water dripping from the exterior termination is weak or has stopped entirely
  • A musty smell from the vents when the blower starts
  • Gurgling from the air handler cabinet as the system runs

What Our Drain Service Covers

We clear the line under vacuum from the termination rather than blowing the blockage further in, flush the pan, and verify flow with a measured volume of water rather than assuming. Then we look at why it blocked: trap geometry, line slope, the access tee, the condition of the secondary pan, and whether the float switches are present, wired and actually working.

Where the piping is at fault we re-pipe it correctly, because clearing a badly routed line simply books the next call. A drain problem often travels with other issues, so we check the coil while we are in the cabinet. A filthy coil sheds debris straight into the pan, and a frozen coil dumps far more water than the drain was sized for, which is covered on our evaporator coil page. Duct leakage pulling humid attic air into the return raises the condensate load too, addressed under ductwork repair and sealing.

Preventing the Next Blockage

Once the line is clear and correctly piped, keeping it that way is straightforward: a cup of distilled vinegar at the access tee every month or two through the cooling season, filters changed on a real schedule, and a seasonal maintenance visit that includes a proper drain flush rather than a glance at the pan. If a drain fails on a weekend and shuts your system down in peak heat, our emergency service covers Jersey Village and the surrounding suburbs, and if the shutdown turns out to be electrical rather than a drain, our compressor and condenser repair service picks it up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did my AC shut itself off with water in the pan?

A float switch did its job. When the primary drain clogs and water backs up into the pan, the switch opens the control circuit and stops the system before the water reaches your ceiling. The air conditioner is not broken, the drain is blocked, and restarting it without clearing the drain simply refills the pan.

How much does clearing a clogged AC drain cost in Houston?

A straightforward clear and flush typically runs $150 to $300 in the Houston metro. Re-piping a badly routed line, adding a proper trap or replacing a failed float switch adds to that, generally landing between $300 and $600 depending on access.

How often do Houston condensate drains clog?

More often than almost anywhere else, because our long humid cooling season means the line carries water for eight months a year and the biological slime that causes blockages grows continuously. Annual clearing is a realistic minimum here, and twice a year suits systems that have blocked before.

Can I pour bleach down the drain line myself?

Bleach is harsh on the metal drain pan and glued joints, and it will not shift an established blockage further down the line. Distilled vinegar at the access tee is gentler and helps as maintenance, but a line that is already backed up needs clearing under vacuum from the termination end.
